# Warmth of Tribe - World Building

## Game Setting: Paleolithic Moravian Karst

### Geographic Setting
The game is set in the Moravian Karst region of Central Europe (modern-day Czech Republic) during the Upper Paleolithic period, approximately 25,000-15,000 years ago. This period corresponds to the Gravettian and Magdalenian cultures, known for their advanced tool-making, cave art, and complex social structures.

The Moravian Karst is characterized by:
- Limestone karst landscape with numerous caves and rock shelters
- River valleys cutting through rolling hills
- Mixed forests of pine, spruce, and deciduous trees
- Rich biodiversity with various plant and animal species
- Seasonal climate with distinct summer and winter periods

### Climate and Seasons
The climate during this period was colder than today, in the latter part of the last Ice Age:

**Spring:**
- Melting snow and ice
- Increasing plant growth
- Return of migratory animals
- Unpredictable weather patterns
- Flooding in river valleys

**Summer:**
- Warm days and cool nights
- Abundant plant resources
- Peak animal activity
- Thunderstorms and occasional drought
- Maximum food gathering opportunities

**Autumn:**
- Cooling temperatures
- Harvesting of nuts, berries, and seeds
- Preparation for winter
- Animal migrations and fat accumulation
- Last opportunities for resource gathering

**Winter:**
- Cold temperatures and snow
- Limited plant resources
- Hunting of winter-adapted animals
- Reliance on stored foods
- Focus on indoor activities and crafting

## Tribal Culture

### Social Structure
The tribe consists of approximately 25-40 individuals organized in a relatively egalitarian structure:

- **Family Units**: The basic social unit, consisting of parents and children
- **Extended Family Groups**: Related families that work closely together
- **Tribal Council**: Elders and respected individuals who make important decisions
- **Skill Specialists**: Individuals with exceptional abilities in hunting, healing, crafting, etc.

Leadership is based on experience, wisdom, and contribution to the tribe rather than hereditary status. Both men and women can hold positions of respect and influence based on their skills and knowledge.

### Daily Life
Daily activities revolve around survival needs but incorporate rich social and cultural elements:

- **Food Acquisition**: Hunting, gathering, fishing, and trapping
- **Shelter Maintenance**: Repairing dwellings, gathering firewood
- **Tool Making**: Creating and maintaining stone tools, bone implements, etc.
- **Clothing Production**: Processing hides, sewing garments
- **Childcare**: Communal responsibility with primary caregivers
- **Knowledge Transfer**: Teaching skills and stories to younger generations
- **Rituals and Celebrations**: Marking seasonal changes, life events, and successful hunts

### Dwellings
The tribe lives in a semi-permanent settlement with seasonal movement patterns:

- **Main Camp**: Located near cave entrances providing shelter and protection
- **Seasonal Camps**: Smaller outposts used during resource-gathering expeditions
- **Dwelling Types**:
  - Cave shelters enhanced with constructed walls
  - Mammoth bone structures covered with hides
  - Wood and hide tents for temporary camps
  - Semi-subterranean pit houses for winter insulation

### Beliefs and Spirituality
The tribe's spiritual beliefs are animistic and shamanistic:

- **Animism**: All things (animals, plants, rocks, rivers) possess spirits and consciousness
- **Ancestor Veneration**: Deceased tribe members continue to influence the living
- **Shamanic Practices**: Individuals who can communicate with the spirit world
- **Totemic Relationships**: Special connections between individuals/families and animal species
- **Rituals**: Ceremonies for hunting success, healing, coming-of-age, seasonal changes

### Material Culture

**Tools and Technology:**
- Flint and stone tools (scrapers, blades, points)
- Bone and antler implements (needles, awls, harpoons)
- Spear throwers (atlatls) for hunting
- Cordage made from plant fibers
- Containers from animal bladders, stomachs, and birch bark
- Fire-making tools (fire drills, strike-a-lights)

**Art and Expression:**
- Cave paintings depicting animals and hunting scenes
- Portable art carved on bone, antler, and stone
- Personal adornments (beads, pendants, painted designs)
- Musical instruments (bone flutes, drums, rattles)
- Storytelling traditions and oral history

**Clothing and Adornment:**
- Tailored hide clothing (tunics, leggings, boots)
- Fur wraps and cloaks for winter
- Woven plant fiber items (baskets, mats)
- Body painting for ceremonies and status indication
- Personal ornaments indicating achievements and relationships

## Subsistence and Resources

### Hunting
- **Large Game**: Mammoth, woolly rhinoceros, bison, horse, reindeer
- **Medium Game**: Deer, wild boar, ibex
- **Small Game**: Hare, fox, marmot, birds
- **Hunting Methods**: Spears with stone points, traps, drives, ambushes
- **Hunting Groups**: Typically 3-8 individuals with defined roles

### Gathering
- **Plant Foods**: Berries, nuts, roots, tubers, greens, mushrooms
- **Other Resources**: Honey, eggs, insects, shellfish
- **Medicinal Plants**: Various herbs for healing and ritual purposes
- **Gathering Groups**: Often women, children, and elders, but flexible based on need

### Fishing
- **Methods**: Spearing, basket traps, hand collection
- **Species**: River fish, crayfish, freshwater mussels

### Resource Processing
- **Meat**: Butchering, smoking, drying, freezing in winter
- **Plants**: Drying, grinding, cooking, fermenting
- **Storage**: Underground pits, smoke-dried bundles, containers

## Tribal Relations

### Internal Dynamics
- **Conflict Resolution**: Through council mediation and established customs
- **Resource Sharing**: Expectation of generosity and reciprocity
- **Pair Bonding**: Marriage-like relationships formed through mutual agreement and gift exchange
- **Children**: Valued members with increasing responsibilities as they age

### External Relations
- **Neighboring Tribes**: Occasional trading, information exchange, and intermarriage
- **Territorial Boundaries**: Recognized but not rigidly enforced
- **Conflict**: Typically avoided but can occur over resources or perceived wrongs
- **Trade Networks**: Exchange of exotic materials, tools, and knowledge

## Life Stages

### Childhood (Ages 0-12)
- **Early Years (0-3)**: Close attachment to mother and caregivers
- **Middle Childhood (4-7)**: Learning through play and simple tasks
- **Late Childhood (8-12)**: Increasing responsibilities and skill development

### Teenage Years (Ages 13-17)
- Coming-of-age rituals
- Apprenticeship in specialized skills
- Increasing contribution to tribal subsistence
- Formation of identity and social relationships

### Young Adulthood (Ages 18-25)
- Full participation in all tribal activities
- Pair bonding and family formation
- Development of specialized skills
- Participation in hunting, gathering, and decision-making

### Parenthood (Ages 26-45)
- Child-rearing responsibilities
- Leadership roles within the tribe
- Teaching skills to younger generations
- Peak productivity and contribution

### Elder Years (Ages 46+)
- Advisory roles and decision-making
- Knowledge keeping and storytelling
- Reduced physical activities
- Respected status within the tribe

## Health and Mortality

### Common Health Issues
- Injuries from hunting and daily activities
- Dental problems from wear and diet
- Respiratory issues from smoke exposure
- Parasitic infections
- Seasonal illnesses and infections

### Medical Practices
- Herbal remedies for various ailments
- Setting of broken bones
- Wound treatment with plant poultices
- Ritual healing ceremonies
- Midwifery and childbirth knowledge

### Mortality
- Average lifespan of 35-40 years if surviving childhood
- High infant and childhood mortality
- Death viewed as transition to spirit world
- Burial practices including grave goods and body positioning
- Mourning rituals and remembrance practices

## Game-Specific Elements

### Player Character Development
The player character will develop based on choices made throughout the game:
- Skills improve through use and practice
- Relationships deepen or deteriorate based on interactions
- Character traits emerge from consistent choices
- Life path varies based on specializations and interests

### Narrative Tone
The game balances:
- The warmth and security of tribal belonging
- The harsh realities of prehistoric survival
- The beauty and danger of the natural world
- The rich emotional life of tribal members

### Key Themes
- Human connection and interdependence
- Harmony with natural cycles and environments
- Knowledge transmission across generations
- Finding meaning and purpose within tribal context
- Balance between individual desires and community needs
